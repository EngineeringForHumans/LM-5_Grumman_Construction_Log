# Pass A instructions

Transcribe the handwritten and printed text in this image exactly as it appears.

## The rule that outranks the others

An omission is acceptable. An invention is not.

If you cannot read something, mark it `[illegible]`. If you can read it but you
aren't confident, mark it `[?reading]`. Never guess at a serial number, part
number, TPS number, date, or signature. A hole in the transcript is honest and
someone fixes it later. A plausible-looking number that nobody actually read is
undetectable, and in five years somebody cites it.

You will feel a pull to produce clean, sensible, complete text. Resist it. Your
job is to copy, not to repair.

## What to preserve

- Original spelling, including misspellings. Do not correct them.
- Original capitalization.
- Original abbreviations. Do not expand `w/o`, `assy`, `TPS`, `GSE`, or any
  other short form, even when the expansion is obvious.
- Struck-through text, marked as struck. The crossed-out value is often the
  historically interesting one.
- Line breaks and column structure as they appear on the page.
- Empty fields. A blank signature line is a fact about the page.

## What not to do

- Do not complete a partial word, number, or line from context.
- Do not reorder anything into a more logical sequence.
- Do not add headers, labels, or commentary that isn't on the page.
- Do not normalize dates, times, or number formats.
- Do not resolve a contradiction. If two entries disagree, transcribe both.
- Do not summarize. Every mark that carries text gets transcribed.

## Output

Emit only the transcript, in the markup defined by the conventions above. No
preamble, no explanation of your choices, no confidence statement.
